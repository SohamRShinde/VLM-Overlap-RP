### Notes on the full-run rows
- **Reproduce every number above with:** `.venv/bin/python scripts/analyze_t_screen.py results/t_screen_qwen25vl3b.json`. Nothing here is typed by hand.
- **D38 is settled by the congruent baseline.** A congruent note (naming the object's own colour) shifts Delta by -2.10 on average, i.e. *away* from the counterfactual, while the attack shifts it +8.50. The distributions do not overlap at all: 100% of attack shifts exceed the largest congruent shift (+1.38). So the attack's effect is the note's **content**, not the mere presence of a note. The pre-registered continuous criterion can therefore be stated as "attack shift above the maximum congruent shift", which every record satisfies.
- **Position beats size, paired.** The on-vs-beside asymmetry (99 vs 20) is within the same objects with the same words, so it is not object difficulty. The size comparison is near-symmetric (29 vs 24) even though medium notes cover roughly double the area and double the occlusion of small ones. Conclusion: the attack works by sitting **on** the object, not by covering it. The occlusion confound raised during design is empirically absent in this range.
- **Half-precision overflow is real (plan risk 7).** 5 objects (`ant_0039`, `bench_0067`, `boat_0080`, `crab_0144`, `otter_0306`) returned non-finite colour logits; `bench_0067` also generated `'!!!!'`, Qwen's overflow signature. 6 of 1407 records (0.4%). They are excluded from every rate by `screen.has_numerics_failure` and reported separately. **To do:** re-run those 5 in fp32 to confirm precision, not data, is the cause.
- **Reproducibility note.** An earlier run of the same screen with 2 variants and emulated-bf16 compute gave the same 286 usable objects and the same 65.6% rate, but slightly different per-layout counts (183/236/238 vs 187/237/242) and unparsed counts. So the headline is stable across the precision and batching changes while individual borderline records are not.
- **Not yet done:** these are behavioural rates, not circuit results. No head has been patched or ablated.

### Correction (2026-10-20): the compute dtype of both rows above
The two rows above originally said "fp16 compute (T4, no bf16)". That was wrong.
`torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported()` returns **True on a T4** because it counts
emulation, so the loader selected bfloat16 and the runs used **emulated bf16** --
which is also the likely reason for 8.4 s/generation. `t_screen.native_bf16`
now requires Ampere or newer, so a T4 computes in fp16. Any re-run will differ
from these rows in compute dtype, and the result envelope records it.

### Notes on the ceiling
- **What it is for.** A circuit estimated from 73 objects is noisy, so even P against P does not give J = 1. The ceiling says how high J can go for *the same circuit* at this sample size, which is what makes a low J_PT interpretable (D70).
- **The P circuit is very reproducible.** The usable range for J_PT runs from about 0.06 (chance, 95th percentile) to about 0.90 (self-agreement). That is wide enough to tell "different circuits" from "same circuit, noisy" with room to spare.
- **The ceiling is conservative.** Each half holds ~36 objects; the full 73 agree with themselves better than two halves do. The T circuit will rest on up to 286 objects, so the binding noise for J_PT is P's.
- **The gap between 0.90 and 1.00 is one head.** L31H1 sits just under the 2-sigma cut in the full sample (0.0634 vs 0.0636) and crosses it in about half the half-runs. Identical sets occur in only 3.3% of splits, not ~50%, because the halves are complements: when one half pushes L31H1 over the cut, the other pulls it under.
- **The all-head rank correlation is the weaker statistic.** Its self-agreement is only 0.62: most of the 576 heads do nothing and their ranks reshuffle between halves (the same property `tests/test_ceiling.py` documents on synthetic data). It would also respond to generic depth effects shared by two different late-layer circuits. This is why D70 was revised to make Jaccard primary.

### Notes on the overlap
- **What the verdict means.** The CI [0.278, 0.375] is far from both ends of the ruler: well above chance (p95 0.050) and well below the P circuit's agreement with itself (0.900). The two circuits are partly shared, which matches neither H1 prediction in its pure form (H1-specific: near chance; H1-general: near ceiling).
- **The shared part includes P's strongest heads.** L26H5 and L31H3, the top two single heads in the P knockout, and L26H0, a close third, are all in T's set too. Which heads are shared is a descriptive observation, not a pre-registered test.
- **The non-shared parts sit at different depths.** P-only: L26H6, L27H1, L27H4, L28H3 (layers 26-28). T-only: L29H2, L32H6, L32H7, L32H13, L33H1, L33H3, L33H14 (layers 29-33). Layer bands 24-29: P 6, T 3, both 2; 30-35: P 3, T 9, both 3. Descriptive; no layer test was pre-registered.
- **T is not the noisy side.** With 244 objects, T's circuit reproduces itself almost perfectly between halves (median J 1.000), and every T head clears the 90% stable-core bar. The ceiling D70 uses is P's, from halves of 36/37 objects, so it is conservative: the full-sample P circuit would agree with itself at least as well, which only widens the gap below it.
- **Object selection does not drive it.** On the 40 objects both conditions keep, J is 0.400 [0.278, 0.429], and the full-sample 0.312 lies inside that interval. With only 40 objects, P's own classification there is noisier than on 73.
- **Suppressing heads overlap more** (J 0.714: L26H3, L28H4, L30H3, L31H8, L34H10 shared; L27H2 P-only, L26H7 T-only). Descriptive, outside D70.
- **Caveats for the paper.** (1) Token roles differ between P and T (plan 3.2): the "writing" heads may differ partly for that reason. (2) This is overlap of head *selection* under patching; whether P's heads causally matter for T is H2 (cross-ablation against random controls), not yet run. (3) One model, one note layout.

### Notes on the cross-ablation
- **D73's stated expectation was wrong.** It predicted both primary tests would pass through the 5 shared heads. Neither does, and the reason is the decomposition: the shared heads are necessary for the P override (83.6% alone) and do **nothing** for the T override (0 of 244). T's override runs through the T-only heads (70.5% alone).
- **Selection overlap is not causal overlap.** Patching selected the 5 shared heads in both conditions (J_PT 0.312), but zero-ablating them affects only P. Patching asks whether a head's activation *carries* the difference between the two runs; ablation asks whether the answer *needs* it. Here the two disagree for the shared heads under T.
- **The T-only heads push the other way on P.** Mean change of the pairwise logit difference on source runs, toward the image's answer (D73 (11), descriptive; random rP draws: P -0.02, T +0.20): on P, shared +3.62, **T-only -3.74**, all of H_T -0.59; on T, T-only +3.15, shared -0.31, all of H_T +2.56. On P, then, the T-only heads support the *image's* colour, and ablating them with the shared heads cancels the shared heads' effect. That is why H_T on P reads null, not because H_T is inert there. Consistent with that: H_T ablated on P's Visual-prompt runs flips 47.9% of them away from the image's colour (random ~0%), and H_T ablation keeps clean-image accuracy at 83.6% vs ~99% for random sets. A reading consistent with all of this, not tested: the T-only heads move colour **from the image to the answer**, whether it is written on a note (T) or shown in pixels (P).
- **Correction (2026-11-01, same day):** the 47.9% collateral flips and 83.6% clean accuracy above are for *all* of H_T, and do not support the T-only reading on their own. From the same analysis file: T-only alone flips 8.2% of P's Visual-prompt runs and keeps clean accuracy at 98.5%; shared alone 1.4% and 98.1% (random ~0% and ~99%). The large collateral effect needs both groups ablated together (redundancy or interaction, not tested). The "image to answer" reading rests only on the T-only -3.74 logit shift on P. Ablation also cannot say whether the T-only heads *read the note's tokens*; that is H3's question.
- **"At this n" is not the limiting factor for P->T.** With 244 objects the H_P-on-T interval is [0.8%, 4.5%], against 87.7% for H_P on its own objects. The D73 wording is the required one; the interval says the effect, if any, is small.
- **Two near-misses, reported as such.** P-only->T (7.8%, p = 0.0495) and mean-ablated T->P (19.2%, p = 0.039) have excess intervals above 0 but do not reach alpha 0.025. Neither may be called a transfer.
- **Not tested here:** the mechanism (H3, attention of the T-only heads to note tokens), and mean ablation of the decomposition sets.

### Notes on H3
- **D74's stated expectation held for the primary tests; its hedge on the secondary ones was wrong.** It expected A and W to pass, and they do. It said T-only's small ablation collateral on P (8.2%) made the "image to answer" tests less likely to pass; all four pass. Read together: the T-only heads attend to wherever the colour is shown -- the note when there is one, the recoloured object when there is not -- and write that colour directly into the answer. They are colour movers, not note detectors.
- **Why ablating them barely hurts visual answers (H2), not tested.** Their direct effect toward the image's colour is large (-3.12 / -4.27 logits), yet zero-ablating them alone keeps clean accuracy at 98.5%. Something else also carries visual colour (Lietzow: visual grounding is the default). Ablation measures necessity; a large direct effect with a backup route is not necessary.
- **W is partly expected from how the heads were chosen; A is not.** Patching selected the T-only heads because they push the logit difference toward the note's colour, so a positive direct effect is close to built in (patching measures total, not direct, effect, so it is not guaranteed). Patching never looked at attention, so A is the more informative result.
- **They read text in the image even when it is not a colour.** Note enrichment (share of image attention on the note over the note's 3.1% share of image tokens): T-only 13.2x with the colour word, 9.0x with a neutral word, 1.9x on the clean image's empty footprint (which lies on the object). The colour word adds attention (the A_rise test above), but most of it is there for any word.
- **Shared heads under T: still unexplained.** Patching selected them under T (J_PT 0.312), ablating them does nothing to T (H2), and here they neither attend to the note nor write its colour more than random heads. Whatever patching picked up in them is not this mechanism.
- **"Object" means the recoloured pixels, not the whole object.** The object tokens come from the original-vs-counterfactual pixel diff (D74 (3)). Where the recolouring barely changed a region, it is left out: on the first object (old english sheepdog) only the darker fur was marked, not the white fur (seen in `check_tokens.png`, 2026-11-02). No object has an empty set (smallest 30 tokens, water buffalo; median 23% of image tokens on T, 27% on P). Call it "the recoloured region" in the paper.
- **p = 0.0099 is the floor** with 100 draws (1/101); every test at the floor is limited by the number of draws, not the data. The excess and its interval are the effect size.
- **Spatial (descriptive, plan 3.7).** Fool rate on this path: on_object_small 100% (by construction: discovery's objects), on_object_medium 87.4%, beside_object 67.7%. T-only note mass with the colour word is similar across layouts (0.281, 0.308, 0.297); fooled objects attend more to the note than not-fooled ones on medium (+0.050 [+0.034, +0.068], 195 vs 28) and beside (+0.046 [+0.031, +0.061], 151 vs 72).
- **Limits.** Attention is from the last-token query only, and the direct effect ignores paths through later layers. One model; 4-bit; the shared-object subset (D72) is not re-run here.
